Literature Search Strategy and Search Records
Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare: From Predictive Models to Generative, Multimodal, and Agentic AI
This repository contains the literature search records used to support
the narrative review:
Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare: From Predictive Models to
Generative, Multimodal, and Agentic AI
The repository is provided to improve transparency regarding how the
literature underlying the review was identified and organized.
Review Design
This study was conducted as a structured narrative and integrative
review rather than a systematic review or meta-analysis.
Given the breadth of healthcare artificial intelligence, a single
universal search query was not considered appropriate. Instead, targeted
searches were conducted separately for the major sections and specific
clinical or methodological questions addressed in the manuscript.
Databases
Literature searches were conducted using:
- Web of Science Core Collection
- Scopus
- PubMed
  
Database-specific search records and exported results are provided in
the corresponding section folders.
Search Strategy
The search process followed the conceptual structure of the manuscript.
Separate targeted searches were performed for major AI paradigms,
clinical applications, methodological issues, and emerging areas.
The principal search domains included:
Section 1 --- Introduction and Background
Searches addressing landmark healthcare AI literature, clinical
translation, and recent developments in emerging AI paradigms.
Section 2 --- Evolution of Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare
Searches covering machine learning, deep learning, transformers,
foundation models, generative AI, multimodal AI, and agentic AI.
Section 3 --- Predictive AI
Targeted searches addressing:
- Risk prediction and prognosis
- Diagnostic performance
- External and prospective validation
- Clinical utility and translation
Section 4 --- Generative AI
Targeted searches addressing:
- Large language models and clinical reasoning
- Clinician--AI comparisons
- Hallucination and factual reliability
- Clinical safety
- Real-world clinical deployment
- Multimodal and vision--language applications
Section 5 --- Multimodal AI
Targeted searches addressing:
- Multimodal clinical data integration
- Vision--language models
- Multimodal versus unimodal performance
- Incremental clinical value
- Data fusion
- Missing modalities
Section 6 --- Agentic AI
Searches focused on agentic AI and AI-agent applications in healthcare,
including systems involving reasoning, planning, tool use, and
multi-step clinical workflows.
Section 7 --- Trustworthy AI and Governance
Searches addressing trustworthy AI, governance, safety, transparency,
fairness, robustness, and related clinical implementation
considerations.
Section 8 --- Clinical Applications
Clinical-domain-specific searches were performed to identify primary
studies demonstrating the application and translation of AI across
healthcare specialties.
Section 10 --- Emerging Frontiers
Searches addressed emerging directions and developing AI paradigms
relevant to healthcare and biomedical research.
Evidence Selection and Prioritization
The searches were intended to identify literature relevant to the
specific scientific questions discussed in each section rather than to
produce an exhaustive systematic evidence inventory.
Where available, greater interpretive weight was given to primary
studies providing stronger evidence of clinical translation, including:
1. External validation studies
2. Multicentre studies
3. Prospective clinical evaluations
4. Comparative and randomized studies
5. Human--AI and workflow evaluations
6. Clinical implementation studies
7. Studies reporting clinically relevant outcomes
Methodological papers, consensus documents, reporting guidelines, and
authoritative reviews were additionally used when necessary to establish
conceptual and methodological context.
Evidence from retrospective or proof-of-concept studies was interpreted
according to its corresponding level of clinical maturity and was not
considered equivalent to prospective evidence of clinical utility.

Within these folders, database exports are identified by database and
search topic. The original search records have been retained where
possible to provide an auditable record of the literature-identification
process.
Interpretation of the Search Records
The presence of a record in the database search results does not
necessarily indicate that the study was cited in the final manuscript.
Studies were selected narratively according to their relevance to the
specific question under discussion, methodological quality, clinical
relevance, and contribution to understanding the development or
translation of healthcare AI.
Accordingly, the repository should be interpreted as documentation of
the literature-search process supporting a narrative review, rather than
as a PRISMA-style systematic-review dataset.
Reproducibility
The search records are made publicly available to increase
methodological transparency and allow readers to examine the literature
pools considered for the major sections of the review.
Because bibliographic databases are continuously updated, repeating the
same search at a later date may produce different record counts.
